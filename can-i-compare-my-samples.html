4 Can I compare my samples?

The question: before comparing conditions, are the samples themselves comparable — or is one wildly different? The plot that answers it: the distribution of counts per sample.

First, reshape the counts to tidy form (one row per gene × sample) and attach the sample info:

counts_long <- counts %>%
  pivot_longer(-c(gene_id, gene_name), names_to = "sample", values_to = "count") %>%
  left_join(meta, by = "sample")

A boxplot per sample, coloured by lifestyle (counts are very skewed, so we plot them on a log scale):

ggplot(counts_long, aes(x = sample, y = log10(count + 1), fill = lifestyle)) +
  geom_boxplot() +
  coord_flip() +
  labs(x = NULL, y = "log10(count + 1)")

If the boxes line up at similar heights, the samples are comparable. 👍

📌 Principle: skewed data (like counts) needs a log scale, or one huge value flattens everything else.

👉 Your turn

Show the same information as a density plot instead of boxplots: one curve per sample, coloured by lifestyle. (Hint: geom_density() with aes(group = sample).)

Solution 
ggplot(counts_long, aes(x = log10(count + 1), group = sample, colour = lifestyle)) +
  geom_density()
